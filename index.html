    Recording takes two button presses

    3DS Recorder is a homebrew for Luma3DS that records the whole console: games, the HOME Menu and apps, both screens, with sound. No capture card, no PC.

    The console turns each recording into an MP4 by itself. This page does the same job on a computer, which is handy once the files are off the SD card or when you want a bigger picture.

    • Blue for a moment: the recorder is ready
    • Red, breathing: recording
    • Green twice: saved
    • Red three times: something went wrong, usually a full SD card

    1. Press R + SELECT

      Anywhere: in a game, in the HOME Menu, in an app. The light breathes red while it records. You can even close the 3DS Recorder app and it keeps going in the background.

    2. Press it again to stop

      Two green blinks and the clip is on the SD card.

    3. Copy the .r3d to your computer

      Recordings live in /3ds/recorder/recordings/. Next to each .r3d the console writes an .mp4 of its own. In the Gallery, press Y on a clip for a QR code that sends it to your phone over Wi-Fi.

    4. Convert it here

      Choose the file at the top of this page, pick the screens, the size and whether to keep the sound, then press Convert.

    What's inside an .r3d

    A recording is a long list of small records: a picture of one screen, a slice of sound, the next picture. The 3DS stores its screens sideways, one column at a time, so the file does too.

    An Old 3DS has a 268 MHz processor and an SD card to keep up with, so the recorder uses whichever of six picture formats it can finish in time. Mostly that means saving only the pixels that changed.

    Open a file and the table fills in with what's in yours.

    FormatHow it worksIn your file
    XR32Lossless. Keeps only the pixel pairs that changed since the last frame.–
    XR32 + HuffmanLossless. The same, packed a little tighter.–
    Q565Lossless. A whole picture on its own, for scenes where everything moves.–
    C420Lossy, like video. A whole picture at half the size, for when the processor can't keep up.–
    XOR-RLELossless. The older way of saving changed pixels.–
    RawUncompressed frames.–
    SoundStereo 16-bit samples. Silence isn't stored, so gaps in time are quiet.–

    Each colour is that format's share of the file's bytes.

    Questions

    The short answers.

    Does my recording get uploaded?

    No. This page has no server behind it. Reading the file, decoding the frames and encoding the MP4 all happen in your browser, using its built-in video encoder. Close the tab and everything is gone.

    Why is the .r3d so much bigger than the MP4?

    The recorder has to keep up with a running game on a small processor, so it writes frames in formats that are quick to save instead of small: up to about 320 MB per minute in 3D games. The MP4 works out to around 10 MB per minute.

    Why are there black bars?

    The top screen is 400 pixels wide and the bottom one is 320. With both stacked, the bottom screen sits in the middle with 40 pixels of black on each side. Choose Top only or Bottom only to export one screen.

    Which size should I pick?

    1× is the real 3DS resolution: 400 × 480 for both screens. 2× and 3× scale it up with sharp pixels, which looks much better on phones and when a site re-compresses the video. The file gets bigger too.

    There's no sound, or it won't play

    Which audio formats a browser can encode depends on the browser and the system. Where AAC is available the MP4 uses it, and it plays everywhere. If it isn't, this page saves the sound as Opus inside the MP4, which most modern players handle and a few older ones don't. The note under the options says which one you'll get.

    It says my browser can't convert

    Use a recent Chrome, Edge or Safari on a computer. The converter relies on WebCodecs, the browser's own video encoder, and some browsers and older phones don't have it yet.

    What if a recording was cut short?

    It converts as far as it goes, and the page tells you. If the power died mid-recording, open 3DS Recorder on the console once first: it recovers the file the next time the app opens.

    The 3D depth slider doesn't do anything

    Correct. Recordings are flat. Move it anyway and watch the title.
